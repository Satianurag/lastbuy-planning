## Why the enterprise case opens the video

The $1.44 million proposed commitment immediately establishes the buyer's decision and its consequence. The component-specific service amendment establishes 22,000 protected units before the viewer sees a reduced order. The quantity bridge then explains 8,000 eligible stock + 4,000 confirmed inbound + 10,000 purchased units. At the example's assumed $80 price, the purchase is $800,000 and the modeled commitment difference is $640,000.

The onsemi discontinuance notice and dated distributor observations remain useful corroboration that the problem category is real. They belong in the supporting material and optional questions, not the opening calculation. They concern a different component, currency and deadline; inserting them into the enterprise decision would imply a relationship the evidence does not establish. The public price calculator is deterministic and does not demonstrate the Foundry workflow. Its September 20 observation is not a September 22 accepted quote. Sources and precision remain in [public market evidence](11-public-market-evidence.md), including the [manufacturer notice](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/8865/PD27281ZA.pdf) and [India distributor listing](https://www.digikey.in/en/products/detail/onsemi/AP0202AT2L00XPGA0-DR/7221063).

## The memorable proof and its exact boundaries

Show the recorded supply summary beside the structured check: the summary states **4,800 current units**, while `SAP-01:quantity` observes **6,000** and quotes the opening count. The current validator rejects that captured response. Approval and export remain unavailable. The lesson is that persuasive prose is not sufficient evidence for a financial action. This demonstrates a captured-response validation fix, not a claim that the model was retrained or that all future mistakes are detectable.

The failure case contains a fresh supply response and reused engineering, service and commitment results from another session. The UI must not describe it as one new four-role run over one immutable snapshot. Stored provider metadata and the current case validation are different layers and must remain visibly separate. See [novel live acceptance evidence](../evidence/live-acceptance-novel.json) and [acceptance summary](../evidence/live-acceptance-summary.json).

The clean displayed case has no completed approvals or export. Cloud export proof comes from separate integration cases against the ERP simulation: an unsafe historical-stock fixture returns HTTP 409 with zero external rows; normal and lost-response recovery fixtures each produce one requisition, with a stable repeated receipt. Use the recorded evidence without implying a new export during recording. See [historical gate](../evidence/cloud-historical-gate-verification.json) and [export/recovery](../evidence/cloud-export-verification-LIVE-20260920.json).

For questions about a corrected stock quantity: an 11,200-unit arithmetic deficit is not itself a valid order in that fixture. The quote's 1,000-unit order multiple leads to a **12,000-unit purchase** under the solver check. Governed input correction, rerun and fresh approvals are still required; the application does not silently substitute a new authorized quantity.

| [SiliconExpert](https://www.siliconexpert.com/by-role/engineering/) | Component lifecycle, availability, alternate and compliance intelligence, with BOM and alerting tools. | Treat reliable component intelligence as a potential input; don't portray established tools as simple searches. |

The defensible proposition is LastBuy's demonstrated connection from component-specific source meaning to structured facts, deterministic quantity checks, version-bound human authority and recoverable export. Public product pages cannot establish that competitors lack the same capability or cannot be configured to provide it. A fair pilot compares the same conflicting cases against the incumbent workflow, measuring resolution time, unsupported assumptions and missed constraints.
